Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is Fanbu a real person?

Yes! Every family gets a dedicated concierge: a real person who knows your parents & owns complex tasks like payment and coordination. We have our AI assistant to provide instant answers for quick tasks & questions. When something needs your concierge, we'll say so and you or your parents can ask for a person anytime.

2. Why can't I just use an AI assistant for this?

Fair question, and it's where I started with my mother-in-law. AI assistants would provide instant answers (eg. “find the best prices for a specific brand of laundry detergent”), but she got stuck when prompted to reset her password, take over the browser, and confirm payment. AI assistants empowered her to start, but not enough to finish. Fanbu handles everything from start to finish, so your parents never get stuck halfway.

3. How does Fanbu's pricing compare to other senior concierge services?

Two models currently exist. (1) By the hour, you're paying $40–80 per hour. The meter runs every minute, even the unproductive ones, like waiting on hold. (2) By the project, eg. finding a home aide or sorting out an insurance problem. Costs vary by the project but can go up to thousands of dollars. With Fanbu, we charge one flat monthly price. Big jobs and small ones included, no surprise bills, no wondering if a request is “worth it.” We strive to build a relationship with your family so that we can better address their needs in the long run.

4. How do you handle passwords and sensitive information?

We will always start with options that do not require handling sensitive information. For example, for patient portals or insurers, we will always ask to be approved as a caregiver so that (a) we can log in as ourselves and (b) we can be removed at any time you or your parents see fit. If a task needs something more sensitive, we will ask only for that task through an encrypted password manager and promptly delete it when you ask.

5. Is there anything you don't do?
  1. We don't provide medical, legal, or financial advice. We can connect you to licensed professionals and offer perspectives depending on the ask.
  2. We're not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 911 first, then let us know and we'll take care of the follow-up.
  3. We're not an in-home care service, so we don't come into the home for hands-on care like bathing, dressing or meals. But we can find, vet and schedule a trusted home care provider for you.
6. What does Fanbu mean?

Fanbu comes from the Chinese word 反哺 (fǎnbǔ), which translates to "feeding in return." It comes from an old legend (not scientific fact) about young crows that grow up & bring food back to their aging parents. In Chinese culture, it represents duty: a duty to give back the care we were given. That's what we help families do.

Our founding story

A Story about Empowerment & Agency

This is me, and this is my mother-in-law. She's 70, doesn't speak much English, and with the exception of taking photos & WeChat'ing her friends, doesn't use technology.

When my wife and I welcomed our first child, she flew in & stayed at our house for a few months to take care of us. During that time, I got to know her better, and one thing that always struck me was all the things she wanted to do, like traveling the world and finding a good deal on baby clothes. But she was bottlenecked since she always had to rely on others: the forms, the calls, the bookings, anything in English, anything with technology.

So I built a prototype: connecting AI agents (with me in the loop) in a language she understands and an app she already uses. Her first query: finding the best cruise tickets from Miami to the Caribbean (which she had apparently been eyeing for a while).

Within minutes, her eyes lit up. It was magical, and the possibilities were excitingly endless. She would ask me: "can it do this and this?" to which I'd respond: "why don't you ask it?". I could see that it just clicked but more importantly, I saw her reclaim her empowerment and agency, something that she never had since immigrating to the US. At that moment, I knew I had to start Fanbu.
